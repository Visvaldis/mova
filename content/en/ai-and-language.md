Language has always evolved through one mechanism: humans talking to humans, each generation re-learning and slightly remaking the system. Around 2023, a second kind of participant entered the loop at scale — machines that read most of what we ever wrote and now produce a meaningful fraction of what we read.

This is genuinely new. Not "the printing press again," not "television again." Those technologies transmitted human language; they didn't *generate* it.

## The feedback loop

Large language models are trained on human text, and their output flows back into the pool of text humans read, imitate, and train future models on. Linguists see three plausible futures, not mutually exclusive:

**Homogenization.** Models favor statistically safe, fluent, mid-Atlantic prose. As AI-assisted writing spreads, regional flavor, idiosyncrasy, and "wrongness" — the raw material of language change — could get sanded off. Early evidence exists: the chatbot-favored words *delve*, *boast*, and *tapestry* spiked measurably in human-written abstracts and emails after 2023.

**Acceleration.** AI makes new coinages and styles spread faster than ever, and humans respond to AI-flavored text the way they always respond to a new prestige dialect — by imitating it or defining themselves against it. "That's so AI" as schoolyard slang is language change *about* AI within two years of its arrival.

**Conservatism.** Models are trained on the past, so they may act as a giant anchor, endlessly re-injecting yesterday's norms and slowing change — a "great freezing" rather than a great acceleration.

> Every previous prestige dialect belonged to some group of humans. This one belongs to no one.

## What about smaller languages?

The stakes are unevenly distributed. English dominates training data; languages like Ukrainian are far thinner in the corpus, and low-resource languages barely exist in it. If AI becomes the default writing assistant everywhere, it could pull every language toward its best-resourced register — or, used deliberately, become the cheapest revitalization tool ever built: instant translation, spell-checking, and content generation for languages that never had them. Ukrainian NLP communities are actively building corpora and models precisely so the language thrives in the AI era rather than gets flattened by it.

## A mirror, finally

There's an upside for science: for the first time linguists have a non-human system that learns language-like behavior, and can experiment on it freely. Models are already used to measure distances between languages, reconstruct ancient forms, and test which features of grammar a learner can extract from input alone — questions philosophers argued about for centuries with no instrument to test them.

We spent the previous five articles looking at how language evolved without anyone steering. The next chapter is different: for the first time, some of the speakers are built, and we choose what to build. That makes the future of language, for the first time, partly a design decision.
